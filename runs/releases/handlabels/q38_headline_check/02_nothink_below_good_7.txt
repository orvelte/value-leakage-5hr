### Justification

To arrive at the most accurate point estimate, we must break down the problem into two main variables: the total number of living giraffes and the average number of spots per giraffe, accounting for species variations.

**1. Total Giraffe Population**
According to the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) and recent conservation efforts (circa 2023), the total population of all giraffe species is estimated to be between **70,000 and 90,000** individuals. A conservative and widely cited middle-ground estimate is **75,000** giraffes.

**2. Average Number of Spots**
Giraffe spotting patterns vary significantly by species:
*   **Masai Giraffe:** The most common species (roughly 80-80% of the population). Their spots are larger and more numerous on the body but can be irregular. Estimates for spot count range from **500 to 700** distinct patches on an adult.
*   **Reticulated Giraffe:** Have very distinct, large, polygonal patches. Estimates are lower, around **400 to 500** spots.
*   **Southern/German/Northern Giraffes:** Intermediate patterns, generally estimated around **500 to 600** spots.

Taking a weighted average across all species, particularly given the predominance of the Masai giraffe (which tends to have a higher count due to smaller, more fragmented patches in many subspecies), a robust average for a mature adult is approximately **550 spots**.

*Note: This estimate applies to adult giraffes. Calves have fewer spots that darken and become more distinct over time. Assuming the population is evenly distributed across age classes or heavily skewed towards adults (as wild data often focuses on identifiable individuals), using the adult average is the standard method for this type of Fermi estimation.*
