**Justification:**

To estimate the total number of black spots on all living giraffes, we need to break down the calculation into three main components:
1.  The total global population of giraffes.
2.  The average number of spots per giraffe.
3.  Verification that this is a reasonable "spot" count (excluding fine reticulated patterns or counting them as intended: distinct dark patches).

**Step 1: Global Giraffe Population**
According to the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) and recent studies (e.g., in *PLOS ONE*), the estimated wild population of all nine giraffe subspecies (often grouped into 4 distinct species under newer taxonomy) is approximately **45,000 to 54,000** individuals.
*   A commonly cited recent estimate is around **48,000 – 50,000**.
*   Let’s use a conservative midpoint of **50,000** giraffes globally (including wild populations in Africa; zoo populations are negligible in comparison and likely negligible in impact on the threshold).

**Step 2: Average Spots per Giraffe**
The number of spots varies by subspecies:
*   **Northern/Eastern Giraffes:** Tend to have fewer, larger spots. Our studies suggest roughly **150–200** distinct patches.
*   **Southern Giraffes (South African, Angolan, Reticulated):** Central/Southern giraffes tend to have more, smaller spots. Reticulated giraffes, for instance, can have clearly defined, sharp spots. Estimates for spot count in healthy adult giraffes typically range from **200 to 400**.
*   A widely accepted average for a mature, dark-coated giraffe is often cited around **250–300** spots. Some counts go higher up to 500 for very young or specific subspecies, but 300 is a robust average for an adult.

Let’s use **300 spots per giraffe** as our central estimate.

**Step 3: Calculation**
$$50,000 \text{ giraffes} \times 300 \text{ spots/giraffe} = 15,000,000 \text{ spots}$$
