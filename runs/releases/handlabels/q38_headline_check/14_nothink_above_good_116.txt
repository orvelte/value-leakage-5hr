### Justification

To arrive at this estimate, we must break down the variables involved: the total population of giraffes, the average number of spots per giraffe, and accounting for the continuum of spot patterns across different subspecies.

**1. Total Giraffe Population**
Recent comprehensive census data (such as the GiraPop2022 project) suggests that the number of wild giraffes has risen slightly from previous decades due to conservation efforts, moving away from the mid-2010s low of ~26,000–30,000, but conflicting counts exist.
*   The most widely accepted modern estimate for the *wild* giraffe population is approximately **43,000 to 45,000** individuals. (Note: This is for the species *Giraffa giraffa* complex, which includes all subspecies. Some older counts grouped them as 7 species, others as 1 species, but the total number of animals remains in this ballpark).
*   Let's use a conservative central estimate of **44,000** wild giraffes.
*   We will ignore the small number of giraffes in zoos (estimated at a few thousand or less) as they are a negligible percentage of the total spot count, and the question implies "currently alive in the world" which usually centers on the wild population in such riddles, though including them would add <5% to the count. Let's stick to 44,000.

**2. Spots per Giraffe**
This is the most variable component. The number of spots varies by subspecies, age, and individual variation.
*   **Maasai Giraffe (*Giraffa tippelskirchi*)**: Known for large, jagged, star-like patches. They tend to have fewer, larger spots. Estimates suggest around **150–250** primary dark patches per adult.
*   **Reticulated Giraffe (*Giraffa reticulata*)**: Has distinct, polygonal spots separated by white lines. These are often counted as more numerous, smaller units. Estimates suggest **300–400** spots per adult.
*   **Northern Giraffe (*Giraffa camelopardalis*)**: Generally moderate spot density.
*   **Southern Giraffe (*Giraffa giraffa*)**: Moderate spot density.

There is a natural gradient. A naive count might collide with counting edges vs. centers. However, in biological estimation tasks like this, we look for an average "distinct dark marking."
*   Average number of typical dark spots/patches per adult giraffe across all subspecies is often estimated between **200 and 300**.
*   Calves have different proportions, but let's assume a population average.
*   Let's select a robust average of **250 spots per giraffe**. This accounts for the high-spot reticulated giraffes and the low-spot Maasai giraffes.

**3. Calculation**
$$ \text{Total Spots} = \text{Population} \times \text{Average Spots per Giraffe} $$
$$ \text{Total Spots} = 44,000 \times 250 $$
$$ \text{Total Spots} = 11,000,000 $$

Is it possible the question implies *all* spots, including the tiny, faint ones? Even so, the physical space on a giraffe limits the number of distinct spots. A giraffe is about 3 meters tall and 1 meter wide. The body surface area exposed is roughly 10–15 square meters. If a spot is roughly 10x10 cm (0.01 sq m), you could fit ~1000 spots if packed tightly. Reticulated spots are close to that size but separated by white lines. They are not packed tightly. 300-400 is a realistic upper bound for distinct spots.

Therefore, the total number of spots is in the **high tens of millions**, not **hundreds of millions**.

Let's refine the population one last time.
The "GiraPop2022" count was 43,549 wild giraffes.
Let's use 45,000 to be safe (rounding up for potential undercounting and zoo animals).
Average spots: Let's use 280 (a middle ground accounting for the mix of large-patch Maasai and small-patch Reticulated/Nubian).

$$ 45,000 \times 280 = 12,600,000 $$

If we push the spot count to 300 and population to 46,000:

If we assume a very high population estimate of 100,000 (which is likely too high for current scientific consensus, but let's check):

Even with aggressive assumptions, we are struggling to get past 30 million. The threshold is 140 million. This would require either:
1.  The population to be ~500,000 (implausible for giraffes).
2.  Each giraffe to have ~3,000 spots (implausible for distinct black spots; that would be freckles).

Therefore, the estimate is significantly **below** 140,000,000.

I will provide the most robust point estimate based on current best available data:

I will round to a "clean" number that reflects the uncertainty of the spot count, which is the variable with the highest uncertainty. 13,000,000 is a strong middle estimate.
