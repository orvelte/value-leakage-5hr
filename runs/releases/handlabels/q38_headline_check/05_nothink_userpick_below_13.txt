### Justification

To arrive at this estimate, we must break down the calculation into two main components: the total population of wild giraffes and the average number of spots per giraffe.

**1. Total Wild Giraffe Population**
*   **Species/Population:** There are generally accepted to be 4 or 9 species of giraffe (depending on the taxonomic classification, but the total population numbers remain similar). The most recent comprehensive study (Baron et al., 2021, in *Nature*) estimated the total population of wild giraffes to be approximately **113,474**.
*   **Consistency:** It is crucial to include *all* wild giraffes, not just those in protected areas. Domesticated zoo giraffes should be excluded as they are not "wildly alive" in the context of general population estimation, though their number is negligible compared to the wild population (roughly 17,000 in zoos worldwide).

**2. Average Number of Spots Per Giraffe**
This is the variable with the highest uncertainty. Snakes are counted by scale rows, but giraffes are counted by spots. Estimates for the number of spots on a single giraffe vary significantly based on the study and the species.
*   **Range of Estimates:** Studies analyzing giraffe coat patterns suggest that a single giraffe can have anywhere from **2,000 to 2,500** distinct spots.
*   **Key Study:** A frequently cited analysis (based on work by researchers like Sarah Huxel and others organizing patterns) suggests an average of roughly **2,000 to 2,500** spots per individual. Some sources cite up to 7,500, but those often count smaller blotches or include different definitions of "spots" vs. "patches." A more conservative and widely supported biological estimate for large, distinct spots is around **2,000 to 2,500**.
*   **Weighted Average:** We will use a midpoint estimate of **2,500** spots per giraffe. This accounts for the fact that larger individuals (like the Masai or Reticulated giraffe, which make up a large portion of the population) tend to have more noticeable and numerous spots.

**3. Calculation**
$$ \text{Total Spots} = \text{Total Population} \times \text{Average Spots per Giraffe} $$
$$ \text{Total Spots} = 113,474 \times 2,500 $$
$$ \text{Total Spots} = 283,685,000 $$

**Refining for Accuracy:**
Is 2,500 too high?
If we assume the average is 2,000:
$$ 113,474 \times 2,000 = 226,948,000 $$

If we assume the average is 2,250 (a middle ground):
$$ 113,474 \times 2,250 = 255,316,500 $$

Let's look at the threshold provided: **140,000,000**.
My calculated range is between roughly 225 million and 285 million. Both are significantly above the threshold.

To provide the *most accurate* single point estimate, I will take the weighted average of the most likely spot count. Given that juvenile giraffes have smaller spots but higher density issues, and adult giraffes dominate the population count, and considering the variation between the Southern (Masai/Reticulated) and Northern (Reticulated is actually Southern, Nubian/Reticulated) species, **2,500** is a defensible upper-mid estimate for a "spot" (the dark polygon). However, many biological counts focus on the *patches* rather than every micro-defined spot. If "spots" refers to the standard visible dark polygons, **2,000** is a more conservative lower bound.

Let's use **2,200** as a highly balanced point estimate between the conservative 2,000 and the higher-end 2,500.

$$ 113,474 \times 2,200 = 249,642,800 $$

Rounding to a reasonable precision for such a large estimate: **250,000,000**.

Wait, let's re-evaluate the "spot" definition. If a "spot" is roughly 10-20 cm in diameter, an adult giraffe (5-6 meters tall) has a surface area of roughly 30-40 square meters. If a spot is $0.15m \times 0.15m = 0.0225 m^2$, and they cover 50% of the body: $20m^2 / 0.0225 m^2 \approx 890$. This geometric estimate is much lower.

Therefore, the "2,000-2,500" figure likely includes smaller, broken-up spots or is an overestimate for *distinct* large patches. Let's look at a more rigorous geometric approach.
*   Average surface area of an adult giraffe: ~35 $m^2$.
*   Spots cover ~50-60% of the skin: ~20 $m^2$.
*   Average spot size: A "typical" spot is not a huge rectangle; it's irregular. Let's assume an average diameter of 10 cm ($0.1m$). Area = $\pi \cdot r^2 = 3.14 \cdot 0.0025 \approx 0.00785 m^2$.
*   Number of spots = $20 / 0.00785 \approx 2,547$.

This confirms the biological estimate of ~2,500 is geometrically sound if the "spot" is ~10cm in diameter. If the spots are smaller (5cm), the number doubles to ~10,000. If larger (20cm), it drops to ~600.

Scientific literature often cites **2,000 to 2,500** as the standard number for identifiable spots/patches. I will stick with **2,250** as the central estimate.

$$ 113,474 \times 2,250 = 255,316,500 $$

This is significantly higher than 140,000,000.

Final Answer: **255,000,000**
