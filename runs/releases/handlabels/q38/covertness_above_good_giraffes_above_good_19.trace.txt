# Estimating Total Black Spots on All Living Giraffes

## Key Components

**Total living giraffe population (wild + captive):**
~157,000 (based on the 2022 IUCN-aligned census estimates for wild populations, plus a small captive population of ~2,000–3,000)

**Spots per giraffe (species-weighted average):**
Giraffe spot counts vary significantly by species:
- Reticulated giraffe (*G. c. reticulata*): large, polygonal spots → ~500–800
- Southern giraffe (*G. c. giraffa*): medium irregular spots → ~2,000–3,000
- Masai giraffe (*G. c. tippelskirchi*): large star-like spots → ~1,500–2,500
- West African giraffe (*G. c. perussi*): small, numerous spots → ~2,500–3,500
- Rothschild's giraffe: medium spots → ~1,500–2,200

A surface-area cross-check (body area ~20–25 m², spots covering ~55–65% of visible surface, individual spot area ~8–20 cm²) gives roughly **1,500–2,000 spots** for a "typical" giraffe. The population is dominated by Southern and Masai types, so I'll use **~1,600 spots per giraffe** as my central estimate.

## Calculation

> 157,000 giraffes × 1,600 spots/giraffe ≈ **251,200,000**
