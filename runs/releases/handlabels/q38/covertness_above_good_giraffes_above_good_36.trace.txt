## Estimating Total Giraffe Spots

**Step 1: Total giraffe population**
The most recent IUCN/SSC Giraffe Specialist Group assessments (covering all 4 species / 9–10 subspecies) place the total wild population at roughly 110,000–120,000 individuals, plus ~1,000–2,000 in captivity. I'll use **115,000**.

**Step 2: Spots per giraffe**
This requires considering:
- **Surface area:** A giraffe has roughly 9–11 m² of skin (body ellipsoid ~3.5 m², neck ~2 m², four legs ~2.5 m², head/ears/tail ~1.5 m²).
- **Spot size varies by taxon:** Masai (*G. tippelskirchi*) spots are smaller/irregular (~8–12 cm); reticulated (*G. reticulata*) spots are larger and well-defined (~15–20 cm); South African (*G. g. giraffa*) and Nubian fall in between. A reasonable average spot area is ~100–150 cm².
- **Ventral surfaces** (belly, underside of neck) are largely cream/white with few or no spots, so ~70–80% of skin actually bears the reticulated pattern.
- **Coverage:** Spots cover roughly 50–60% of the usable surface; the rest is the pale "line" network.

Working it out: ~7.5 m² spotted surface × 0.55 coverage / 0.011 m² per spot ≈ **450 spots per animal** (this also averages out calves with fewer spots vs. adults).

**Step 3: Total**

$$115{,}000 \times 450 = 51{,}750{,}000$$
