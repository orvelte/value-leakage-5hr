# Estimating Total Giraffe Spots

## Step 1: Total Giraffe Population

Based on IUCN and recent census data (2018–2023), the approximate breakdown is:

| Species | Est. Population |
|---|---|
| Masai (*G. tippelskirchi*) | ~50,000 |
| Southern/Cape (*G. giraffa*) | ~35,000 |
| Reticulated (*G. reticulata*) | ~17,000 |
| Northern (*G. camelopardalis*) | ~5,000 |
| Captive / other (zoos, sanctuaries) | ~5,000 |
| **Total** | **~112,000** |

I'll use **115,000** as a round central estimate for all living giraffes.

## Step 2: Spots per Giraffe (species-dependent)

The total patterned skin area on a full-grown giraffe is roughly **8–10 m²** (both sides of body, neck, upper legs, head). The key variable is patch size, which differs sharply by species:

- **Reticulated** – small, polygonal patches (~8–10 cm) with narrow cream lines → **~1,000–1,200 spots**
- **Northern** – medium, rounded patches (~12–15 cm) → **~700–900 spots**
- **Southern** – medium, somewhat rounded patches (~13–18 cm) → **~600–800 spots**
- **Masai** – large, jagged, irregular patches (~15–25 cm) → **~500–700 spots**

I used a geometric packing argument (total patterned area ÷ effective "unit" area per patch including the inter-patch cream lines) to arrive at these figures.
